Cua Spaces has two things to know: **machines** and **Spaces**. A machine
provides Spaces. A Space is the isolated sandbox where work happens.

| Name | What it is | Where you see or use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Machine | A Mac, Windows or Linux computer you added to your account, by signing in on it or with **Add a machine**. It can provide Spaces, share its own desktop, or both. | The **Machines** tab. `--on host:<machine-id>` creates new Spaces on it. |
| Space | An isolated sandbox: an image plus a kind (`container`, `vm` or `microvm`; containers use gVisor where installed). See [Space kinds](/spaces/concepts/space-kinds). | The **Spaces** tab. `local:<name>` on this machine, or its own `relay:` id when it runs on another machine, with `host` naming the machine that runs it. A machine you added by address (set up with `--direct`, no relay) has a `direct:<host:port>` id. |
| A machine's shared desktop | The machine's own screen, input, shell and files, shown as a Space while **Let other machines control this desktop** is on. It is not a sandbox. | `relay:<machine-id>` for a machine set up on the relay. A direct host is its own `direct:<host:port>` Space, added with `cua spaces add <host:port> --host`. |

## One list of machines

Sign in on a machine and it joins your account. Every machine you own appears
in the **Machines** tab on all the others. There is no second list to keep in
step: a machine's state is a line on its row, and what you can do to it is on its page.

The state on a row is one of these:

- **Online** or **Offline**.
- **Desktop shared**, or **Desktop and 2 Spaces** when it also provides Spaces.
  The shared desktop is not counted among the Spaces.
- **Signed in, not sharing**: you signed in on it, but it neither shares its
  desktop nor provides Spaces. **New Space** never offers it as a place to run.
- **Waiting for you**: a machine asking to join your account. Its page asks
  **Add this machine?** with **Add machine** and **Not me**.

Machines you have not seen for 30 days collapse into **Offline for 30+ days**,
and machines your team shares appear under **Team machines**.

Open a machine to rename it, to turn its two switches on or off, to see the
Spaces on it, to press **New Space here**, to read its **Details**, or to remove
it with **Remove from account**. Another machine's switches are read only: change
them on that machine.

## What a machine provides

Two switches decide what a machine does, and each works on its own:

- **Let other machines control this desktop** shows the machine's own screen as
  a Space for your other machines.
- **Let other machines create Spaces here** lets your other machines, and
  teammates you share it with as editors, create sandbox Spaces on it.

A machine that only provides Spaces keeps its own desktop private. A machine
that shares nothing is simply signed in.

## One machine, two records

Behind the list, a machine can have two records: the relay's record of a machine
that shares its desktop or provides Spaces (set up with **Add a machine** or
`cua host setup`), and the record of a sign-in on it (made by `cua auth login`
or the app). They describe one machine, and the **Machines** tab joins them into
one row when it can match them safely, for example by the same id or matching
names. If the match is unclear, such as two Macs with the same name, they stay as
separate rows. The `cua devices` commands manage the sign-in record, and
`cua machines rm` removes a machine that is gone.

A machine that only hosts, set up with `--direct` or with its own token, can't
reach your other machines: signing in is what lets a machine see them.

See [Add a machine](/spaces/guides/add-a-computer) for the two switches and how
to add one, and [How Spaces works](/spaces/concepts/how-spaces-works#space-ids)
for Space ids.
