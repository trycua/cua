---
title: Share a game server
description: 'Share a TCP service in a Space, such as a Minecraft server, at a public address anyone can connect to.'
---

A game server speaks plain TCP, not HTTP, so a share link cannot carry it.
A public TCP share gives it a public address instead: a host and port people
connect to, and for known games on Cua's relay a name they can type, such as
`mc-1a2b3c4d.m.cua.sh` for Minecraft.

<Callout type="info">
  Public TCP shares are an experiment. Turn on **Public TCP shares** in
  Settings, Labs. Until then every TCP share is refused.
</Callout>

## Share a Minecraft server

Run the server in a Space (for example `mc`), then share its port:

```bash skip="no-runner"
cua share space:mc:25565 --tcp --public --service minecraft
```

The command prints the address to type in Minecraft's "Server Address", and
one line saying how it is reached ("Through your AWS account" or "Through
Cua"). Players need nothing from Cua. `cua share ls` lists the share, and
`cua share stop <id>` ends it at once.

From an agent inside the Space, the same works with the Space's own port:
`cua share 25565 --tcp --public --service minecraft`, or the
`share_tcp_port` tool of the Space's MCP server. The agent needs Child Spaces
turned on for that Space, because it asks through the same narrow channel.
An agent's share waits for you first (see [Approval](#approval)).

## How people reach it

Each share takes one of three routes, picked when it is made:

| Route | When | What people connect to |
|---|---|---|
| Your cloud host | The Space runs in your linked cloud | The Space's own host: its address and one port opened for this share |
| Your share server | The Space runs on your computer and a cloud is linked | One small server in your cloud, reused for every share; your computer connects out to it |
| Cua | No cloud is linked | Cua's relay, with the limits below |

With a cloud linked, the traffic of your shares stays between your players,
your cloud and your computer. The address is your cloud's own (an IP address
and port, or the server's cloud host name); it does not depend on Cua's DNS.

To pick a route yourself, add `--route host`, `--route edge` or
`--route relay`.

### Your cloud host

A Space in your linked cloud runs on its own host. A share opens exactly
one TCP port of that computer's Cua firewall, to the addresses you allow, and
the Space serves only that port. The firewall rule carries Cua's tags and the
share's end time; it closes when the share ends, and a sweep closes any rule
a crash left behind. Spaces made before this feature need to be made again to
serve shares this way.

### Your share server

The first share through a cloud asks once:

> This creates a small server in your AWS account (about $7 a month plus
> $0.09 per GB players download). Remove it any time.

Say yes in the app, answer `y` to the command, or pass `--yes`. Cua then
makes one small server in your account with its own address and its own
certificate (no Cua keys or certificates are copied to it), reuses it for
every later share, and removes it after three days without shares, or when
you remove it:

```bash skip="no-runner"
cua share settings --remove-edge
```

The server runs nothing but the share relay: no SSH, security updates
installed automatically, every port closed except the share ports and the
one your computer connects out on. Your computer dials it; nothing in your
network is opened.

### Cua

Without a linked cloud, Cua's relay carries the share. You can turn this off
so shares only ever go through your own cloud:

```bash skip="no-runner"
cua share settings --land only-my-cloud
```

## Who can connect

By default anyone with the address can connect. To limit a share to some
addresses, pass them (repeat `--allow`, addresses or CIDR ranges):

```bash skip="no-runner"
cua share space:mc:25565 --tcp --public --service minecraft \
  --allow 203.0.113.7 --allow 198.51.100.0/24
```

On your cloud host the firewall rule itself only opens to those
addresses. `cua share settings --allow ...` sets the list new shares start
with.

For Minecraft Java, `--check-host` also drops connections that did not dial
this share's address, such as port scanners.

## Approval

A public share an agent asks for waits for you. It shows in the app's Shared
links with **Approve** and **Turn down**, and in `cua share ls` as `waiting`:

```bash skip="no-runner"
cua share approve share-1a2b3c4d5e6f
# and let that Space share without asking from now on
cua share approve share-1a2b3c4d5e6f --always
```

An agent never agrees to a share server's cost for you: a share that needs
one waits for you either way. Turn approval off with
`cua share settings --approve-agents off`.

## Daily data cap

Public shares stop for the day once they moved the daily cap (2 GB unless
you change it), so a busy server cannot run up your cloud's data bill:

```bash skip="no-runner"
cua share settings --daily-cap 5GB
cua share settings --daily-cap off
```

`cua share settings` shows the cap, what was used today, where shares go and
the share server, if there is one.

## What a TCP share can reach

- Only the one port of the one Space it names. It never reaches this
  computer, your network or another Space. An agent can share ports of its
  own Space and the Spaces it made, nothing else.
- Admin and remote-access ports are refused: SSH, RDP, VNC, the Minecraft
  console (RCON, 25575), Docker and Kubernetes APIs, and common databases.

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| How long a share lasts | 24 hours, or less with `--ttl` |
| Shares at once | 2 per tree of Spaces; on Cua's relay also 2 per account |
| New connections from one address | 2 per second, bursts of 10 |
| Players at once | 64 connections per share |
| Speed (Cua's relay) | 4 MiB/s per share, both directions together |

A share ends when it expires, when you stop it, when the day's data cap is
reached, or when its Space (or the Space whose agent made it) is deleted. If
the computer serving it goes offline, the address stops working within ten
minutes.

Every share, approval and stop is in your audit log, with who made it and
its route.
