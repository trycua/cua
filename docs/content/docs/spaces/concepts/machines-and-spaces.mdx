Cua Spaces has two things to know: **computers** and **Spaces**. A computer
provides Spaces. A Space is the isolated sandbox where work happens.

| Name | What it is | Where you see or use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Computer | A Mac, Windows or Linux computer you added to your account, by signing in on it or with **Add a computer**. It can provide Spaces, share its own desktop, or both. | The **Computers** tab. `--on host:<machine-id>` creates new Spaces on it. |
| Space | An isolated sandbox: an image plus a kind (`container`, `vm` or `microvm`; containers use gVisor where installed). See [Space kinds](/spaces/concepts/space-kinds). | The **Spaces** tab. `local:<name>` on this computer, or its own `relay:` id when it runs on another computer, with `host` naming the computer that runs it. A computer you added by address (set up with `--direct`, no relay) has a `direct:<host:port>` id. |
| A computer's shared desktop | The computer's own screen, input, shell and files, shown as a Space while **Let other computers control this desktop** is on. It is not a sandbox. | `relay:<machine-id>` for a computer set up on the relay. A direct host is its own `direct:<host:port>` Space, added with `cua spaces add <host:port> --host`. |

## One list of computers

Sign in on a computer and it joins your account. Every computer you own appears
in the **Computers** tab on all the others. There is no second list to keep in
step: a computer's state is a line on its row, and what you can do to it is on its page.

The state on a row is one of these:

- **Online** or **Offline**.
- **Desktop shared**, or **Desktop and 2 Spaces** when it also provides Spaces.
  The shared desktop is not counted among the Spaces.
- **Signed in, not sharing**: you signed in on it, but it neither shares its
  desktop nor provides Spaces. **New Space** never offers it as a place to run.
- **Waiting for you**: a computer asking to join your account. Its page asks
  **Add this computer?** with **Add computer** and **Not me**.

Computers you have not seen for 30 days collapse into **Offline for 30+ days**,
and computers your team shares appear under **Team computers**.

Open a computer to rename it, to turn its two switches on or off, to see the
Spaces on it, to press **New Space here**, to read its **Details**, or to remove
it with **Remove from account**. Another computer's switches are read only: change
them on that computer.

## What a computer provides

Two switches decide what a computer does, and each works on its own:

- **Let other computers control this desktop** shows the computer's own screen as
  a Space for your other computers.
- **Let other computers create Spaces here** lets your other computers, and
  teammates you share it with as editors, create sandbox Spaces on it.

A computer that only provides Spaces keeps its own desktop private. A computer
that shares nothing is simply signed in.

## One computer, two records

Behind the list, a computer can have two records: the relay's record of a computer
that shares its desktop or provides Spaces (set up with **Add a computer** or
`cua host setup`), and the record of a sign-in on it (made by `cua auth login`
or the app). They describe one computer, and the **Computers** tab joins them into
one row when it can match them safely, for example by the same id or matching
names. If the match is unclear, such as two Macs with the same name, they stay as
separate rows. The `cua devices` commands manage the sign-in record, and
`cua spaces rm` removes a computer that is gone, and keeps its Spaces on your account (detached) unless you pass `--delete-spaces`.

A computer that only hosts, set up with `--direct` or with its own token, can't
reach your other computers: signing in is what lets a computer see them.

See [Add a computer](/spaces/guides/add-a-computer) for the two switches and how
to add one, and [How Spaces works](/spaces/concepts/how-spaces-works#space-ids)
for Space ids.

## Your default Space

Your account can name one Space as its default: where "my Space" goes when you do not say. The
relay keeps it, so the app, `cua`, your phone and your agents all agree. If the default is
offline, they try the order you set (or, by default, every online computer, oldest first), and
then this computer. `cua spaces default` shows which one it picks and why, `cua spaces default
<space>` sets it, and `--clear`, `--fallback a,b` and `--no-fallback` change the rest.
`cua spaces ls --json` and the `list_spaces` tool mark it with `"default": true`, and give each
Space a `parent`: the computer that provides it, or null for a computer.
