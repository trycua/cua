---
title: Cua Spaces on your phone
description: 'Follow your agents from an iPhone or iPad: start a task on one of your computers, read the thread, answer approvals, review changes and watch the desktop.'
---

The Cua Spaces phone app shows your Spaces grouped by the computer they run on.
Each Space opens as a conversation with its agents. You can start a task, send
a message, answer an approval, look at what changed, and open the live desktop
of a Space. Nothing runs on the phone itself: the work stays on your computers.

The phone app is a separate product from the desktop apps. What your computers
do for it is an [experiment](/spaces/guides/experiments) called `mobile`, and it
is off until you turn it on.

## Turn it on

On each computer that should answer your phone:

```bash test="cli-shape" id="phone-on"
cua experiments enable mobile
```

You can also use Settings, Advanced, Labs once the experiment is on. While it
is off, the computer does not answer a phone's requests and does not report
events for alerts. The experiment is the same switch the other experiments use:
`CUA_EXPERIMENTS=mobile` turns it on for one process.

## Sign in

Open the app and sign in with your Cua account. The phone adds itself to
your account, the same way another computer does, and your computers
appear in the list. A computer that is off or asleep shows as offline, and the
last thread you read stays on the phone, sealed, so you can read it without a
connection. Messages and answers you write offline wait in an outbox and are
sent once, in order, when the computer is back.

The app asks for Face ID, Touch ID or the phone's passcode when it opens, when it
comes back after a minute away, and before it opens the desktop of a computer
if you last unlocked more than five minutes ago, so a stolen, unlocked phone is
not instant access. It is on by default; Settings, Privacy turns it off. A phone
with no passcode cannot be asked anything, and Settings says so.

## Which Spaces it shows

Every Space running on a computer that is signed in and sharing is on your
account, so the phone lists it without a step from you, whichever way it was
made: in the Mac app, with `cua spaces create`, from an SDK, by an agent or by
a job. A new Space shows up a few seconds after it runs, and while you are
creating many at once they are listed once you pause. A Space leaves the list when it stops or is deleted, and when you sign
out of the computer. Only your account reaches them, and a Space you have not
shared with anyone stays yours alone.

To keep one Space off your account, make it with `--no-relay`
(`cua spaces create --no-relay`); a child Space follows its parent. To keep all
of them off, turn the setting off. `cua spaces ls` says which Spaces stay off.

```bash test="cli-shape" id="relay-spaces-off"
cua config set relay.spaces off
```

A Space you added by its address (`cua spaces add`) was not made by Cua and is
never listed this way.

## What you can do

- Start a task: write the job, pick an agent and the computer it runs on, and
  optionally a repository. The computer makes the Space.
- Follow a thread: messages, short rows for the agent's tool calls, and the
  status of each agent.
- Answer approvals: Allow, Allow for this Space when the computer says it can be
  remembered, or Decline. When several wait, the card says how many.
- Queue or steer a running agent: a message while it works either waits its turn
  or interrupts it.
- Review changes: the changed files, a unified or word diff, and a preview of
  images and text.
- Dictate a message with the phone's own speech recognizer. No Cua server
  transcribes it.
- Watch the desktop of a Space in the viewer.

A phone cannot run up your computer or a bill. At most three tasks start at once, twenty an
hour, and three new agent homes a day per account. A task from a phone does not start where a
linked cloud could be billed unless you allow it on the computer with `CUA_PHONE_MAY_SPEND=on`.
"Allow for this Space" lasts a day; after that the same request asks again. Approvals for
pushes, sign-ins, joins and abilities are never remembered.

## Watching and controlling a desktop

A computer that shares its desktop shares it as a Space, and the phone opens it
like any Space, with the same roles the desktop app uses. Your own phone may
look and touch. A teammate you shared it with may do what their share allows: a
viewer only looks. There is no extra question on the phone or the computer:
sharing the desktop is the permission. The live desktop in the viewer is view
only until you take control in the app.

What a phone never reaches is the computer underneath: it cannot run a command,
read or write a file, open a tunnel, shut the computer down or create Spaces on
it, whatever it is allowed in a Space. When you stop sharing the desktop, a
phone that is watching it stops within a second.

A computer needs the latest Cua Spaces to be used from a phone. The relay does not send a phone's calls to
one that predates phones, and the phone says "update it". What widens a Space's reach (an ability for a home
Space, an agent asking to join) is answered on the computer, not from a phone. "Allow for this Space" from a
phone lasts a day and names the phone that gave it.

To make a computer answer no phone at all:

```bash skip="host-install"
cua-spacesd phone off
cua-spacesd phone on
```

To remove one phone, remove it from the Computers list: that ends its sessions,
its viewer tickets and its alerts. To remove every phone of your account at once, use
Remove all phones in the app's Settings (it asks for Face ID or the passcode, and a recent sign-in).
Resharing or removing a computer from a phone also needs a sign-in made in the last ten minutes, and a
phone never adds a computer. What phones did is a line each in the log
(`cua-spacesd phone audit`), and the computer's access log names the phone and
whether it looked or touched.

## Alerts on a closed app

An alert is sent when an agent needs you or finishes. It says only that, with the
agent's name: never message text, tool output or file content. Alerts need the
relay that connects your account to be set up for them.

If you run your own relay, turn alerts on with `CUA_RELAY_PUSH=on` and give it
the Apple push key through `CUA_PUSH_APNS_KEY_FILE`, `CUA_PUSH_APNS_KEY_ID` and
`CUA_PUSH_APNS_TEAM_ID`. Keep the key out of the image and out of source
control. A relay without `CUA_RELAY_PUSH=on` behaves exactly as it did before:
the push routes answer 404, and the viewer is handled as it always was. The
same setting also lets the phone's web view open the viewer, with the relay
serving the viewer page itself inside a sandbox. See the relay README for the
full list and a deploy checklist.

## Build it yourself

The app is in `apps/cua-spaces-mobile` (React Native with Expo and a Rust core).
Its `README.md` explains how to build it for the Simulator, and `docs/SETUP.md`
lists what a release needs: an OAuth client for the phone, an Apple developer
account, and the push key.
